With all of Western Europe under fascist control in one form or another, Britain was desperately short of trading partners. A nation of maritime trade, Britain was by no means self-sufficient, and depended upon food and supplies from North America.

Nazi Germany immediately identified the weakness, and began a relentless campaign of submarine warfare against merchant shipping coming across the Atlantic. Millions of tonnes of merchant shipping was sunk, with countless thousands of lives lost. The situation quickly became critical.

Merchant ships would travel in convoys which could be protected (to a point) by escorts of naval vessels. The plan proved its worth against U-boats in the First World War and dramatically improved the safety of the shipping, but it needed intense coordination. Much of that day-to-day coordination was conducted from a secret bunker beneath the streets of Liverpool.

Secret for decades, it remains much as it was in World War Two, providing a fascinating experience for us today.

In 1940, Princess Marina, the Duchess of Kent, became Commandant of the Women's Royal Naval Service (WRNS), whose members were affectionately known as Wrens both colloquially and officially. The Princess visited the Western Approaches bunker in May 1943. Born in Greece, it likely pained her to see the land of her birth under Axis occupation.

Her visit was fitting, as the facility was staffed predominantly by women of the WRNS and WAAF (Women's Auxiliary Air Force), often young women aged 18-21.

I highly recommend a visit to the bunker. Opening times and tickets are on the [Liverpool War Museum website](https://liverpoolwarmuseum.co.uk).
