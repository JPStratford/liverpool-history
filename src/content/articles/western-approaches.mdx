With all of Western Europe under facist control in one form or another, Britain was desperately short of trading partners. A nation of maritime trade, Britain was by no means self-sufficient, and depended upon food and supplies from their allies in North America.

Nazi Germany immediately identified the weakness, and began a relentless campaign of submarine warefare against merchant shipping coming across the Atlantic. Millions of tonnes of merchant shipping was sunk, with countless thousands of lives lost. The situation quickly became critical.

In *******, American ******* proposed a shift in tactics whereby ships would travel in covoys which could be protected (to a point) by escorts of naval vessels. The plan dramatically improved the safety of the shipping, but it needed intense coordination. That coordination was not conducted from London or Washington; it was conducted from a secret bunker beneath the streets of Liverpool.

Secret for decades, it remained undisturbed until the 1990s, and is perfectly preserved to provide a fascinating experience for us today.
